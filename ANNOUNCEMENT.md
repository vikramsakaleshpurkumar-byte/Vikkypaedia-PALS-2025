# Announcement — Paediatric Advanced Life Support 2025 (v4, 2026)

## Substack post

Most children who arrest do not have heart disease. They run out of oxygen, or out of circulation, over hours that someone could have seen. When the arrest comes, it is usually a slow heart rate sliding into asystole, not the ventricular fibrillation that dominates adult teaching.

The 2025 resuscitation guidelines changed several things we teach:

- The two-finger technique for infant compressions is gone.
- In asystole, epinephrine should go in as early as possible.
- In VF, the shock comes first.
- After the arrest, fever is the enemy.
- For the first time, the guidelines say how to judge whether a child will wake up, and what survivors need afterwards.

I have rebuilt my paediatric advanced life support module from those published guidelines. It has 20 units, and every unit has a role note for students, nurses, PGs and faculty. It is free, works offline on a phone, and says plainly what it is not: it is not the AHA course, and it is no substitute for putting your hands on a manikin.
